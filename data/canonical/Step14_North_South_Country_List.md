# Step 14 Global North and Global South Classification

A socioeconomic and historical classification, not a geographic one: latitude/hemisphere is explicitly excluded. Global South = economies that historically experienced lower levels of industrialization and development and have less influence in the global economic system; Global North = generally higher income, industrialization, infrastructure, human development, and political/economic influence.

Operationalized as: Northern America (United States, Canada) + all of Europe (including Russia) + Australia and New Zealand + Japan + Republic of Korea + Israel + Singapore + Cyprus = Global North; every other economy = Global South. High-income Gulf states (Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Brunei) are classified South despite high per-capita income, since their wealth is resource-based rather than the industrial/historical-development pattern the definition describes.

One contested case, flagged rather than silently decided: Turkiye (TUR) is classified South here (the more common placement in this convention), but it is an OECD member and EU-accession candidate, so a case for North exists.

Canonical population: 184 economies. Global North: 52. Global South: 132.
